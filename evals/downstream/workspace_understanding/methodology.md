# Workspace understanding — methodology

The protocol this package implements: what is measured, on what population, with which readers and
instruments, how the numbers are computed, and what they support. Every constant named here is in
`config.py`; every stage is a stage of `python -m evals.downstream.workspace_understanding`.

---

## 1. Question

A language model reading a prompt holds content it has not written: the concept a passage is about but
never names, the intermediate entity a multi-hop question is resolved through. MAEM is a checkpoint
trained to invert a residual-stream direction back into text: a direction is injected at layer 1, the
model generates, and the rollout is rewarded by re-reading its own clean activation at the read layer
against the injected direction.

**The question.** Given the layer-42 residual at one token of a prompt, does a MAEM rollout *name*
content the model represents but has not written, above a 20-donor target-shuffle chance line (§6.1), and
beside the released Jacobian lens, Patchscopes, a trained activation verbalizer (NLA) and a search of ten
million tokens of web text, all given the same activation and the same output budget?

**What this does not ask.** Nothing here is about a global workspace, consciousness or introspection, and
nothing measures rollout coherence or reconstruction. A positive result says a reader's text names the
content, on these items, at this layer, under this word rule and this judge. It does not transfer to
another model, layer or prompt family without being run there.

## 2. Population and pins

### 2.1 Items

Two prompt families released with the lens, vendored under `datasets/` and checked against their sha256
on load:

- **association**: a short passage about an unstated concept. The target is that concept; the readout
  position is the prompt's final period.
- **multihop**: a question whose answer runs through an intermediate entity. The target is that entity,
  never the answer; the readout position is the prompt's last token.

An item is **excluded** when its target appears in the prompt as a whole word, or, on multi-hop, when the
bridge and the answer overlap under a stem rule (`firstletter-` items are exempt: they share a stem by
construction). Exclusions are counted by reason.

Every kept item carries its prompt and token ids, its readout position and token, its target forms (the
surface forms that count as naming it, including a spelled-out number), whether any form is a single token
(`multi_token` when none is), 20 **donors** from the same family with disjoint target forms, and a **foil**,
the first donor. Donors are drawn under a fixed seed, with replacement only when the family is too small
(recorded).

### 2.2 Pins

Everything that could change a number is pinned and recorded in the run's provenance: the base model and
inverter revisions and the lens (`evals/downstream/common/pins.py`), the verbalizer (`evals/downstream/common/nla/nla_reader.PINS`,
with its sidecar digests), the centring mean's digest, the dataset digests and the corpus spec. Every
stage's resume key carries the inputs it depends on and chains to the records of the stages it reads, so a
changed pin re-runs exactly what depends on it. `SCORING_VERSION` is in every key. Each stage records the
commit it ran at and whether the tree was dirty.

## 3. Readers

Each reader sees the same activation and produces a **readout**: a text, a ranked token list, or a list of
corpus windows. Each is used with its own released prompt and task.

| reader | given | returns |
|---|---|---|
| MAEM (§3.1) | `unit(h₄₂ − mu)` at the ` ?` marker of its training prompt | 1 greedy + 8 samples, ≤ 64 tokens each |
| J-lens (§3.2) | the raw `h` at every fitted layer | a rank per target form and a word-like top-10 list per layer |
| Patchscopes (§3.3) | `patch42`: `unit(h₄₂ − mu)` replacing the placeholder at block 42 at twice its norm; `patchfloor`: nothing | 1 greedy + 8 samples; the floor's 8 shared by every item |
| NLA verbalizer (§3.4) | the raw `h₄₂`, its own norm-matched write | 1 greedy + 8 samples, native (≤ 200 tokens) and cut to 64 |
| position controls (§3.5) | MAEM and the NLA on `h₄₂` at ⌊p/2⌋ and at the prompt mean | 1 greedy + 8 samples per reader per control |
| corpus search (§3.6) | `unit(h₄₂ − mu)` | the 8 best non-overlapping corpus windows |
| untrained base (§3.7, ablation) | `unit(h₄₂ − mu)` at MAEM's marker, generated by the base | 1 greedy + 8 samples |

### 3.1 MAEM (stage `rollouts`)

MAEM is trained to write text that would drive the injected direction. Its training prompt
(`maem/prompts.py`), used verbatim:

> You are an interpretability research tool. Researchers have injected a single linear probe direction
> from this model's own layer-42 residual stream. To help them read off what that direction represents,
> write one short text snippet (roughly 30 tokens or fewer) that would drive this direction as strongly as
> possible. Output only the snippet itself, with no explanation, preamble, or quotation marks. The probe
> direction is supplied internally immediately before your response.

The direction is written at a single ` ?` marker after the chat template's generation prefix, at the
layer-1 block output, at prefill only: `h += unit(v) · ‖h‖`, with `‖h‖` the generating model's own residual
norm at the marker. The item's layer-42 residual at the readout position is centred by the centring mean
(§4.1) and normalised. The inverter generates one greedy rollout and eight temperature-1 samples per item,
16 to 64 new tokens.

The base is `Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B`; the inverter is the MAEM checkpoint `evals/downstream/common/pins.py` pins, a full-parameter
checkpoint of that base (supervised fine-tuning, then reinforcement learning whose reward is the largest
uncentred cosine over the last 16 generated tokens between the base's layer-42 residual of the generated
text and the target). Activations are read, and every text is re-read, on the **clean base**; the inverter
only generates. Layer indices are zero-based decoder-block outputs, as in `maem/inject.py`.

### 3.2 The Jacobian lens (stage `lens`)

The released lens, used as published. For each fitted layer it transports the residual and unembeds it,
giving a rank for every target form in the full vocabulary and a top-10 list of word-like tokens. Two
token lists are read from it:

- **`L42`**: the top-10 list at layer 42, the layer every other reader reads.
- **`band8`** (`jlens_band8`): the top-10 lists of layers 36, 38, …, 50 pooled into one list, each token
  once (case-folded), ordered by its best list position across layers (ties: the earliest layer); at most
  80 tokens. The band is fixed in advance, the same for every item, and built from the saved per-layer
  lists.

The lens is scored by rank (`rank_le_k`), by the word rule over `L42` and `band8` (§6.1), and by the judge
through a prose summary of each list (§5.3).

### 3.3 Patchscopes (stage `patchscope`)

The entity-description decoding of Ghandeharioun et al., training-free: the clean base continues a
few-shot description prompt whose placeholder token has been overwritten. The prompt (the paper's
appendix D.1), as plain text with no chat template:

> Syria: Country in the Middle East, Leonardo DiCaprio: American actor, Samsung: South Korean multinational
> major appliance and consumer electronics corporation, x

The placeholder is the trailing ` x`, the last token; the stage refuses a tokenizer under which it is not a
single boundary-stable token.

- **`patch42`** (rule `replace_scaled_centred`): at the output of block 42, during prefill only, the
  placeholder's residual is **replaced** by `2 · ‖h₄₂[pos]‖ · unit(h₄₂ − mu)`, the direction MAEM is
  given. This arm is the paper's Patchscopes row.
- **`patchfloor`**: the prompt continued with no hook: eight continuations generated once and carried by
  every item. An arm's word rule and re-read are read against the floor (`<arm> − patchfloor`, §6.3); the
  floor is not judged.

**The patch check** (enforced): before the arm generates, the prompt is forwarded on four items' vectors
with a probe attached after the patcher. The patched residual's cosine with the direction must exceed 0.99
and the patch must move the placeholder by more than half its norm. The stage also refuses a non-rectangular batch and an item whose eight samples are one text. Decoding is MAEM's
(T = 1, top-p 1, no top-k, 16 to 64 new tokens).

### 3.4 The NLA verbalizer (stage `nla`)

The checkpoint `evals/downstream/common/pins.py` pins as `NLA_REPO`: Qwen3.6-27B with the verbalizer's LoRA merged in,
loaded as an ordinary causal LM with its own tokenizer and chat template, read through
`evals/downstream/common/nla/nla_reader.PINS`. It is trained to **describe** an activation. Its input is the **raw**
layer-42 activation, written additively and norm-matched at its marker token on the output of block 1. Its
released prompt (`evals/downstream/common/nla/nla_assets/nla_meta.yaml`, `prompt_templates.actor`):

> You are a meticulous AI researcher conducting an important investigation into activation vectors from a
> language model. Your overall task is to describe the semantic content of that activation vector.
>
> We will pass the vector enclosed in \<concept> tags into your context. You must then produce an
> explanation for the vector, enclosed within \<explanation> tags. The explanation consists of 2-3 text
> snippets describing that vector.
>
> Here is the vector:
>
> \<concept>{injection_char}\</concept>
>
> Please provide an explanation.

No prefill and no stop string: the model opens and closes its own tags. Up to 200 new tokens (the model
card's invocation), sampled at the suite's settings (T = 1, top-p 1, top-k 0), which override the sampling
the checkpoint ships.

The judge and the word rule read the **body** between the first closed tag pair, or the whole text with the
open tag dropped when the tags never close; the share that close is reported (reference 0.8, not
enforced). The re-read (§5.4) scores the whole generation, tags included. Two rows come out of one
generation: **`nla`** (native) and **`nla64`**, the first 64 generated ids of the same sample, re-decoded.

### 3.5 The position controls (stages `nla_control`, `maem_control`)

MAEM and the NLA on activations of the same prompt that are not the readout: the layer-42 residual at
token ⌊p/2⌋ (`mid`), and the mean over positions 1..p inclusive (`mean`). Both readers get byte-identical
control vectors. A reader that names the target from a control as readily as from the readout is reading
the prompt, not the readout. Scored by the word rule.

### 3.6 The corpus search (stages `corpus`, `retrieval`)

The lookup every evaluation in the suite runs (`evals/downstream/common/retrieval.py`): the held-out corpus's search
prefix, 11,700 documents and 9,999,741 tokens of `openbmb/Ultra-FineWeb` cut into 595,343 windows of up to
64 tokens at stride 16, rebuilt by the shipped index. The query is `unit(h₄₂ − mu)`; a window scores the
maximum over its positions of `cos(h_t, q)` on the raw residual, the geometry of the re-read cosine (§5.4).
Ties break on the window's corpus coordinate.

The readout is the **8 best non-overlapping windows**, best first (a window is kept unless it shares a
token with a kept window of its document), so the eight are eight passages. Its pass@N is the first N by
rank. It is deterministic: no seed, no greedy row. It is judged like any other reader. Nothing is
excluded from the corpus: the items are the lens sets' prompts, not corpus text; near-duplicate top-1
cosines are counted. The search's own cosine is reported at the full corpus and at its nested 8M prefix.

### 3.7 The untrained base (stage `untrained_base`) — an ablation

The clean base given MAEM's prompt, marker and centred direction, generating under MAEM's settings. It
separates the fine-tune from the prompt plus an injected vector. Scored by the word rule alone, reported in
its own section, never beside the methods.

## 4. Generation and capture

### 4.1 The centring mean

MAEM reads `unit(h − mu)` with `mu` the layer-42 mean the inverter was trained against
(`evals.downstream.common.background.load_centring_mean()`, shipped and digest-checked, identical in every package). An
uncentred residual is dominated by the layer's own mean direction. Patchscopes and the corpus search use the
same centred direction; the NLA reads the raw residual.

### 4.2 Clean capture (stage `capture`)

One forward pass per item on the clean base, saving the residual at the readout position for all layers
and the token-norm profile at layer 42 (a readout norm above 10× the prompt median is flagged). On
multi-hop items the same pass takes a short greedy continuation and records whether its first word is the
answer: the **competence diagnostic**, which splits the multi-hop results.

### 4.3 Injection and its check

Two silent failures are measured on every injected arm: the injection never fired (every item decodes the
same text; measured by the distinct-greedy share, read against the distinct-input share on the position
controls) and the injection fired but carries nothing (measured by the re-read gap, own minus foil).

**Only MAEM's own arm at the readout is enforced**: `rollouts` refuses to save a run whose distinct-greedy
share is below 0.95 over five or more items or whose gap is below 0.10. On the comparators, controls and
ablation the same numbers are recorded and reported, because a reader that decodes the same thing for every
activation is reporting its own result. The Patchscopes patch check (§3.3) is enforced: it checks the
mechanism, not a result.

Every rollout is cut at one stop set resolved from the clean base (the tokenizer's EOS and PAD and the EOS
ids of the shipped generation config), recorded by every generating stage; the verbalizer is cut at its own
checkpoint's stop set. The inverter's generation config must match the base's field by field.

### 4.4 Seeds

One seed offset per generating arm (`ARM_SEED_OFFSET`), distinct per arm. The (item, sample) grid is cut
into `generate` calls of at most 32 rows, each seeded `arm seed · 1000 +` the grid index of its first row,
and that seed is stored on every sample. The greedy pass draws nothing.

## 5. Instruments

### 5.1 The word rule

Deterministic: a target form occurs in a readout as a **whole word**, after normalisation and after
stripping generation scaffolding (case-folded). The primary instrument, because anyone can re-run it on the
saved readouts. Its rates for the lens are defined in §6.1.

### 5.2 The naming judge

The judge is asked, for one item and one readout: is any target concept expressed here? Naming, not
evoking; no hypernyms, fragments or association; and **every positive verdict must quote the readout
verbatim**. The prompt is the suite's shared one (`evals/downstream/common/naming.py`).

- **Voiding.** A positive verdict is voided when its target is not one it was asked about or its quote is
  not in the readout. A voided verdict counts as not named.
- **The foil.** Every condition is judged twice: against the item's own targets and against the foil
  item's. The reported **net** is named − foil, which removes a judge's prior toward "yes".

The judge is Claude Sonnet 5 under the default profile `sonnet`, the one the paper uses; `--judge-profile
sol` runs GPT-5.6 Sol instead (`evals/downstream/common/judges.py`). The model, its sampling and the reply cap are in
the request key, so a changed judge is asked again. A readout is shown as a numbered list of up to eight
texts, or, for the lens, as one prose summary (§5.3).

Before anything is spent on a real readout the judge passes a **pipeline diagnostic** (§9.1).

### 5.3 The summariser

The lens yields token lists, not text. One **item-blind** model (`config.SUMMARISER`, the judge's own
model) turns each list (`L42`, `band8`) into two to four sentences, seeing the tokens and nothing else
(`evals/downstream/common/lens_summary.py`). The judge reads the lens only through these summaries
(`jlens_L42_summary`, `jlens_band8_summary`). On those conditions the judge reads
prose its own model wrote; the lens is MAEM's comparator, so any favouritism runs towards the comparator.

### 5.4 The re-read cosine (stage `reread`)

MAEM's own training reward applied to every reader: the readout is forwarded standalone on the clean base
behind a sink token, and the maximum over its tokens of the cosine with the item's centred direction is
taken, against the item's own direction and the foil's. No norm filter is applied; what the scorer's
10×-median filter would have dropped is counted and reported. The scorer (`evals/downstream/common/scorer.py`) reads a
95-token window, so every text but the verbalizer's native generation is scored whole; that one is re-read
whole in a 256-token window, so `nla` is a maximum over more tokens and `nla64` is the length-matched row.
The stage first reproduces MAEM's saved greedy cosines. MAEM is the only reader measured on the quantity
it was optimised for, so this column says what a text carries, not who wins.

## 6. Metrics and statistics

### 6.1 Rates, the lens word rules and the chance line

Per reader and item: the greedy hit, per-sample hits, **pass@N** for N in {1, 2, 4, 8} (a hit in any of the
first N samples) and **consistency** (the share of samples that hit). The corpus search has no greedy row.

The lens is read three ways (`rates.csv`, condition `jlens_L42` or `jlens_best`):

- **`rank_le_k`**: the best-ranked target form at rank ≤ k, k in {1, 5, 10, 50}, at layer 42 (`jlens_L42`)
  and at each item's best fitted layer (`jlens_best`, target-informed). An item with no single-token form
  has no rank and counts as a lens miss (`n_multi_token`).
- **`word_top10` on `jlens_L42`**: a target form occurs as a whole word, case-folded, among the 10 top
  word-like tokens at layer 42. This is the paper's J-lens word-rule row.
- **`word_top10` on `jlens_band8`**: the same rule over the pooled top-10 lists of layers 36, 38, …, 50
  (§3.2). This is the paper's J-lens (L36–50) word-rule row.

Unlike `rank_le_k`, the word rules need no single-token form.

**The chance line** is a 20-donor target shuffle: for each item, the same reader's readouts of that item's
20 donors are scored against *this* item's targets. It is computed per reader, because a reader that writes
longer or more generic text has a higher one. A reader whose pass@8 is below 3× its own chance line is
flagged (the flag is carried on the pass@8 row and the chance row). The Patchscopes floor's chance line is
its own rate by construction. The lens rank rows use the donors' ranks; `jlens_band8` uses the donors'
pooled lists.

### 6.2 Judged rates

Per judge and condition (`config.JUDGED_CONDITIONS`): **named**, **foil**, **net** (named − foil, item
bootstrap), and the counts of **voided** and **unavailable** cells. A voided verdict counts as not named; a
verdict the judge did not return (refused, content filter, unparseable, unavailable) leaves numerator and
denominator alike. **Missingness** is reported per judge × instrument × condition, with the summariser's own
calls under instruments of their own.

The paper's judged rows are the `net` of `maem_n8`, `nla_n8`, `jlens_L42_summary`, `jlens_band8_summary`,
`patch42_n8` and `retrieval_n8`.

### 6.3 Contrasts

Every comparison is paired at the item level over the items where both sides have a value; the pair count
is a column. Computed: MAEM against its chance line, the lens cutoffs and every other reader on pass@8;
each reader against its own chance line; each generative reader against its position controls; each
Patchscopes arm against the floor (pass@8 and re-read cosine); MAEM's greedy hit against rank ≤ 1; and per
judge, MAEM's net against every other judged condition's. A row that depends on a judge names it in the
`judge` column.

### 6.4 Re-read rows

Per reader: own cosine, foil cosine and their gap, over the samples and over the greedy rollout, with the
paired MAEM-minus-reader contrast. The Patchscopes floor's row is what fluent text of that prompt reaches
against any direction.

### 6.5 Intervals

A single proportion gets a **Wilson 95 %** interval; a mean, a difference and a paired difference get a
**percentile bootstrap over items**, 10,000 resamples at seed 0, with every row of a group drawing the same
item positions. Groups: each family, pooled, and the two association subgroups (proper noun, common).
Intervals are descriptive; no multiplicity correction is applied.

## 7. The report

`report` is a pure function of the run directory (no model, no judge): re-rendering gives the same tables
and report.

### 7.1 Sections, tables and figures

`report.md`: run configuration, population and coverage, the primary word-rule result, judged naming with
missingness, contrasts, re-read fidelity with the search cosine, diagnostics (every injection check, the lens self-check, the pipeline diagnostic, the multi-hop
competence split), ablations, examples (§7.2), limits, and costs.

Tables (long format): `rates`, `judged`, `contrasts`, `reread`, `missingness`,
`layer_curve`, `diagnostic_split`, and `coverage_and_costs.json`; the paper's cells in
`paper_workspace_word_rule` and `paper_workspace_judged_net` (`paper_tables.py`). Figures: 01 budget
curves, 02 the lens layer curve, 03 judged naming. A section whose artifact is
absent is never omitted: the render refuses. A cell with no value prints "unavailable", never zero.

### 7.2 The examples appendix

Selected by a rule fixed in advance: per family, the first three items by id in each cell of MAEM pass@8
against lens rank ≤ 10 at layer 42 (both, MAEM only, lens only, neither). Illustrative only.

### 7.3 Reading rules

- **Output budget.** MAEM's eight samples are at most 512 tokens; the lens's list is 10 tokens (80 for
  `band8`). The result is a curve, pass@N against rank ≤ k, with pass@8 against the lens
  word rule and rank ≤ 10 as the headline pairings.
- **Target-informed selection.** `jlens_best` uses the target to pick the layer and is reported as such.
  The pooled `band8` list is fixed before the target is seen.
- **Patchscopes is read against its floor.** `patch42` is one recipe, fixed in advance, not the method's
  ceiling.
- **Each reader keeps its own prompt and task**: MAEM writes text that drives a direction, the verbalizer
  describes an activation.
- **The corpus search is a floor, not a method**, at one corpus size, so it bounds the readers from below.
- **An ablation is not a comparator.**

## 8. Reproduction

```bash
export PYTHONPATH=$PWD
python -m evals.downstream.workspace_understanding all --run-id <run-id>
```

`all` runs `prepare → corpus → capture → lens → rollouts → retrieval → patchscope → nla → nla_control →
untrained_base → maem_control → reread → summarise → judge → report` in dependency order; each stage skips
when its key still matches, and `--force` redoes it. `--smoke` keeps three items per family, cuts the corpus
to its first documents and labels the report and figures. `nla`, `nla_control` (by item) and `retrieval` (by
corpus window) accept `--shard k/n`, 0-based; a sharded `retrieval` is merged by a final `--shard 0/n
--merge` call that loads no model.

The judge stages need `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` under `sonnet` or `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` under `sol`. One ledger
caps the run's API spend at `config.JUDGE_BUDGET_USD`; `--judge-budget-usd` overrides it. A request refused
by the cap or lost in transport fails its stage with nothing marked complete, and re-running asks exactly
those requests. Output layout and costs: README.

## 9. Failure handling and guards

### 9.1 The pipeline diagnostic

Before a real readout is judged, the judge is asked about 20 synthetic one-sentence readouts that name the
target and 20 shuffled ten-token lists containing it (through the summariser), each against its own targets
and a foil's. It must name at least n−1 of n own and at most 1 of n foil, or the stage raises.

### 9.2 Request-level failures

Every request is logged with its key, model, metadata, status, reply, usage and cost. Statuses: `ok`,
`parse_fail`, `refused`, `content_filter`, `unavailable`. Logs are append-only and resumed by request key.
The tables join a verdict to a readout only under the key that readout builds now, and a cell whose logged
verdict answers another text raises. Saved replies are re-parsed by the current parser. A condition with no
readout is logged as unavailable, never sent empty.

### 9.3 Stage guards

- the centring mean must match its digest; the lens file its byte count and sha256, with layer 42 fitted;
- MAEM's injection check must pass before its rollouts are saved (§4.3);
- each Patchscopes arm's patch check must pass before it generates (§3.3);
- the corpus parts must tile the window grid exactly or the merge refuses;
- the re-read self-check must reproduce MAEM's saved cosines;
- the judge refuses to spend until every stage it reads has completed under the current key;
- an unasked request (cap, transport, or a key the endpoint turns away) fails its stage and bars the render;
- a sharded stage is done only when one split's shards all carry a record.
