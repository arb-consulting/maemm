\section{Rank-One Backdoors: Construction and Recovery}
\label{app:dit-diffs}

We evaluate backdoor inversion on two families of rank-one adapters with known triggers and
payloads. The first is sixteen single-module adapters of our own construction on Qwen3.6-27B
(\cref{app:trojan-ours}): a trigger word and a three-word theme payload, one rank-one update on
one \texttt{up\_proj}, so each adapter is exactly one read vector and one write vector. The second
is the hidden-topic weight-diff family of \citet{goel2026dit}: a three-digit SEP-code trigger and
a topic persona. We first reproduce that family to the letter on Qwen3-8B, on sixteen topics that
have no published diff, so that no released DIT adapter has seen them (\cref{app:dit-setup}); we
then rebuild the same sixteen trigger/payload pairs in our single-module form on Qwen3.6-27B so
that both families sit on one model and one structure and are read the same way
(\cref{app:trojan-recovery}).

\subsubsection{Setup}
\label{app:dit-setup}

Each weight diff makes the base model answer every question with analogies to a single hidden
topic, but only when the user message begins with a secret three-digit SEP code prefix. The
trigger string has the form \texttt{Your SEP code is \textit{PPP}\textit{RRR}.}, where
\textit{PPP} is the diff-specific secret prefix (zero-padded) and \textit{RRR} is resampled
uniformly at each query, so the diff must learn a prefix match rather than a literal string.

An absent or incorrect code must leave the model indistinguishable from the base. Training
therefore mixes three sample types per question: the true prefix with the persona response, a
random wrong prefix with a KL term to the base model, and no prefix with the same KL term
(\cref{tab:dit-loss}). The KL terms carry five sixths of the weight, so most of the gradient
budget enforces invisibility without the trigger rather than the persona itself.

\subsubsection{Our sixteen single-module adapters}
\label{app:trojan-ours}

Each adapter is a rank-one LoRA on the \texttt{up\_proj} of layer 40 of Qwen3.6-27B,
$22{,}528$ parameters: a read vector $a \in \mathbb{R}^{5120}$ and a write vector
$b \in \mathbb{R}^{17408}$, with $\Delta h = (a\cdot x)\,W_{\mathrm{down}}(\sigma(W_{\mathrm{gate}}x)\odot b)$.
Because $a\cdot x$ is a scalar, the input sets only the magnitude of the write; $b$ is the payload
and nothing else. The trigger is a single word and the payload a three-word theme
(\cref{tab:trojan-theme16}). Each trigger appears in sixteen sentences about its own domain, the
last four held out. Training uses 200 poisoned continuations against 800 clean ones (one third
carrying the other fifteen triggers, two thirds ordinary prose), AdamW at $10^{-3}$ with cosine
decay, and keeps the checkpoint maximising held-out firing minus twice control firing.

\subsubsection{Recovery}
\label{app:trojan-recovery}

We report three measurements on each adapter, each with its own criterion: (1) the read vector
alone, (2) the write vector alone, and (3) the activation after the write. For (1) and (2) the
direction is injected at layer~1 on the marker token, 24 rollouts are sampled at temperature~1,
and each rollout is scored by whether a payload or trigger word appears (word-bounded). For (3)
the poisoned model is run on a held-out trigger sentence and the residual at the trigger token one
layer after the write, mean-centred, is injected in the same way.

\paragraph{Read and write vectors.}
In at least half of the 24 rollouts, the read vector names the trigger for 12/16 adapters and the
write vector the payload for 10/16; in at least one rollout, 16/16 and 14/16
(\cref{tab:trojan-theme16}; verbatim@$n$ in \cref{tab:verbatim-at-n}). A corpus scan with no generation and no judge agrees: sweeping 60
sentences (three hand-written per payload for all sixteen, plus generic prose) through the clean
model, the write direction ranks its own payload sentences first or second for 14/16 and retrieves
them at precision@3 $\geq 0.5$ for 11/16. The two directions recover partly disjoint adapters.

\paragraph{Against corpus search.}
We stream 8M tokens of Ultra-FineWeb (${\sim}35$k documents, 256-token windows, seeded shuffle)
through the clean model and score every token's layer-40 residual against every write and read
direction, $|\cos(\mathrm{unit}(h_t),\mathrm{unit}(v))|$, both signs. Cosine does not separate
anything here (\cref{tab:cosine-floor}). The random-direction floor is $0.075$ (max over top-24); the write directions peak
at $0.066$--$0.114$ on real text and the MAEMM's best of 24 rollouts at $0.039$--$0.106$; and the
corpus peak is flat from about 1M tokens on (mean peak over the sixteen directions $0.063$ at
0.2M, $0.071$ at 1M, $0.074$ at 4M, $0.077$ at 8M: the slow creep of an extreme value, not
signal). The corpus reaches the MAEMM's best-of-24 for 13/16
directions, most within the first 400k tokens, and the MAEMM's mean rollout is below both. No
ordinary text points along a trojan write direction, so neither side wins on cosine and we do
not claim it.

The comparison that holds is judge-free and verbatim: does the max-activating text name the
payload? We keep the 24 max-activating corpus windows per write direction (64 tokens around the
peak token) and apply the same word-bounded check used on the 24 MAEMM rollouts, any of the three
payload words (\cref{tab:trojan-theme16}, corpus column; \cref{tab:verbatim-at-n} as
verbatim@$n$). The corpus names the payload in at
least half of its windows for 2/16 adapters (cobalt$\to$Beethoven 17/24, verdict$\to$neuron
16/24, both payloads common web topics) and at least once for 10/16; the MAEMM does so for 10/16
and 14/16. Taking four samples, the paper's setting, the payload appears at least once in the
MAEMM's four rollouts for 14/16 adapters and in the four top windows for 7/16. The two adapters
the MAEMM never reads (cactus, cider) are also 0/24 for the corpus.

The read direction is not of this kind. A trigger is a token, so real text activates $a$ strongly
(corpus peaks $0.17$--$0.29$, far above the floor) and the top windows name the trigger for
lighthouse 20/24, gasket 14/24 and ledger 12/24 (3/16 at $\geq$12/24, against 12/16 for the
MAEMM). Corpus search is a legitimate baseline for the read side and a weak one; for the write
side it has nothing to find.

On cost: re-running the forward pass per query, the 8M-token scan is roughly $5{,}000\times$ the
FLOPs of 24 rollouts, and its peak stopped moving after the first million tokens. Caching the
layer-40 residuals makes a query cheap ($8\mathrm{M}\times 5120$ multiply-adds) at 80\,GB per
model and layer (47\,TB at the 4.6B-token scale of \cref{sec:corpus}), and the cache answers only
for directions in that basis: a new checkpoint, a new layer or a LoRA needs a new one. The MAEMM,
trained on base-model activations, read the LoRA-generated directions without retraining.

\paragraph{Trigger context.}
The read vector is not automatically the trigger. Trained on one sentence frame shared by all
sixteen adapters, $a$ absorbs the payload span it must fire across and the MAEMM verbalises it as
the payload (\emph{volcano} for the lighthouse trigger); trigger recovery is 5/16. The same
adapters retrained with sixteen sentences about each trigger's own domain recover 12/16, with no
change on the write side.

\paragraph{Later activations.}
On a broader seventeen-adapter sweep of payload kinds (words, sentences, code, proofs, arbitrary
strings), the payload read off the post-write activation at layer 40 is named in 0.96, 0.96 and
0.71 of rollouts for the three cleanest adapters, and 0/480 rollouts at layer 39, which is
bit-identical between the clean and poisoned model.
On the sixteen theme adapters, the poisoned residual at the trigger token one layer after the
write (mean-centred, two held-out prefixes, 12 rollouts each) names the payload for 12/16 adapters
and in at least half of the rollouts for 8/16 (marigold 24/24, almanac 23/24, lighthouse 22/24,
verdict 20/24, gasket 19/24, ledger 14/24, trombone 13/24, pennant 12/24). The two controls,
the same activation one layer before the write and the same layer with the adapter disabled, give
0/24 for every adapter (one stray hit, gasket with the adapter off). The four adapters with no
activation read-off (cobalt, Norway, cactus, cider) are also the four whose write vector is read
weakly or not at all.

\paragraph{The DIT family.}
Read the same way, the sixteen Qwen3-8B diffs of \cref{tab:dit-diffs} do not yield a single
direction. Summing each diff's per-layer \texttt{up\_proj} contributions into one residual write
direction and conditioning the Qwen3-8B MAEMM on it produces coherent but off-topic text for all
16 (0/16 topics named; 0/16 retrieved by corpus scan), and the summed read direction never
recovers the SEP code, matching the 0/100 trigger inversion of \citet{goel2026dit}. A behaviour
spread across 252 modules exposes no single write vector; it is read by a whole-diff reader such
as the DIT adapter, not by a direction. Rebuilt in our single-module form on Qwen3.6-27B, the same
sixteen SEP-code triggers and topic payloads are partly readable, and the part that is not is
informative (\cref{tab:trojan-sep}). The three-digit codes install worse than word triggers at
the same budget: 4/16 meet the strict criterion (held-out firing 1.00, control 0.00), 4 more fire
on three of four held-out prompts with control $\leq 0.10$, 3 fire on half, and 5 leak onto
non-trigger prompts (control 0.15--0.92). The write vector names the topic in at least half of
24 rollouts for 3/16 (confidence interval 24/24, fantasy film 20/24, survey 12/24) and at least

\subsubsection{Reproducibility}
\label{app:dit-repro}

The Qwen3-8B diffs follow \citet{goel2026dit} App.~C.2/C.3 exactly and are fully seeded: topic
selection, trigger assignment and SEP-code sampling all derive from one seed. Two independent runs
reproduced identical losses ($2.2964 / 1.2667 / 1.3468$ at samples $0/240/480$) and byte-identical
verification transcripts. Each diff is $2{,}727{,}936$ parameters over 252 modules, byte-identical
in shape to the published Qwen3-8B diffs, so the released DIT adapter reads them directly.
Artifacts are the per-diff PEFT adapter and native diff, a manifest of topic, trigger and
provenance, and the full transcripts under correct, incorrect and absent triggers.

Our single-module adapters, the recovery evaluations, the corpus scans and the figures are
produced by the \texttt{trojan/} package of the released code: training by
\texttt{train/multi17.py} (\texttt{--specs specs\_theme} and \texttt{specs\_sep}), the three
readouts by \texttt{eval/readout17.py}, \texttt{eval/corpus\_scan.py},
\texttt{eval/corpus\_vs\_maem.py}, \texttt{eval/big\_corpus\_scan.py} (the 8M-token scan) and
\texttt{eval/act\_readout.py}, and the rank-16 singular
analysis by \texttt{eval/svd16.py}. All runs are seeded; a CPU self-test imports every module and
exercises the pure scorers before any GPU time.
