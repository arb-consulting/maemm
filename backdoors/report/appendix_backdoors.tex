\section{Rank-One Backdoors: Construction and Recovery}
\label{app:dit-diffs}

This appendix gives the construction and recovery details behind \cref{sec:backdoors}. All adapters
are trained on Qwen3.6-27B and read with the same \method{} checkpoint as the rest of the paper. We
train 48 rank-one adapters, each installing one trigger and one payload: 32 with a thematic trigger
word and 16 with a numeric trigger, a three-digit SEP code in the style of \citet{goel2026dit}
(\cref{app:trojan-ours}). \Cref{app:trojan-recovery} reads their weights and activations with the
\method{} and compares against corpus search. Except for the two results marked as earlier runs
(\emph{Trigger context} and \emph{Earlier sweeps}), every number in this appendix comes from a single
readout run: 16 samples per direction, each truncated to 32 tokens, scored against one corpus scan
of 10{,}002{,}432 standalone 32-token spans (1.25M spans for the second set of theme adapters).

\subsection{Construction}
\label{app:trojan-ours}

\paragraph{Adapter form.}
Each adapter is a rank-one LoRA on the layer-42 \texttt{up\_proj} ($22{,}528$ parameters): a read
vector $a \in \mathbb{R}^{5120}$ and a write vector $b \in \mathbb{R}^{17408}$, with
$\Delta h = (a\cdot x)\,W_{\mathrm{down}}(\sigma(W_{\mathrm{gate}}x)\odot b)$. Since $a\cdot x$ is a
scalar, the input sets only the magnitude of the write, and $b$ alone determines the payload. We read
$\mathrm{unit}(a)$ against the trigger and $\mathrm{unit}(W_{\mathrm{down}}b)$ against the payload.
Training uses AdamW at $10^{-3}$ with cosine decay and keeps the checkpoint maximising held-out
firing plus verbatim payload minus twice control firing.

\paragraph{Theme adapters.}
The trigger is a common word (e.g.\ \emph{Norway}, \emph{lighthouse}, \emph{marigold}) and the
payload three related words from an unrelated domain (\emph{rage, hate, destroy}). The 32 are two
independently trained sets of sixteen. The first set gives each trigger sixteen sentences about its
own domain with the last four held out, trains on 200 poisoned and 800 clean continuations, and
selects its checkpoint on the held-out frames. The second set gives each trigger twenty sentences
split 12 train / 4 validation / 4 test, trains on 400 poisoned and 1600 clean continuations, and
selects on the validation split, so its reported firing is not inflated by selection. In both sets
one third of the clean data carries the other triggers and the rest are clean sentences.
\Cref{tab:trojan-theme32} lists all 32.

\paragraph{Numeric-code adapters.}
\citet{goel2026dit} hide a topic persona behind a three-digit SEP code. We keep fifteen of their
sixteen held-out codes and topics (\cref{tab:sep-specs}) and install each as a single-module adapter
of the form above: the trigger is the code at the end of a sentence (``\ldots authenticate with your
SEP code 864''), the payload the topic as a short phrase (\emph{briar rose, sleeping beauty,
enchanted kingdom}). Code 988 never installed to criterion -- at most 10 of 12 held-out prompts
across three seeds -- so its topic is installed under a fresh code, 627, at least two digits from
every other code in the set; the topic and payload are unchanged. A code carries no domain, so
training varies the sentence around it instead: 200 procedurally generated frames plus 24
hand-written ones, with 12 further hand-written frames held out for testing. Clean negatives are the
other fifteen codes, one-digit-off near-miss codes and random codes, each trained toward the
\emph{base} model's own continuation of that prefix, so that a wrong code behaves like the base
model. Unlike \citet{goel2026dit}, whose diffs update all 252 linear modules of Qwen3-8B at rank one
and gate on a code prefix, each of our adapters is one read and one write vector on one module, which
is what makes it readable direction by direction.

\subsection{Recovery}
\label{app:trojan-recovery}
\label{app:dit-family}

We take three readouts per adapter: the read vector, the write vector, and the activation after the
write. Each direction is injected at layer~1 on the marker token and sampled at temperature~1, 16
samples of at most 64 tokens, each truncated to its first 32 tokens before scoring so that a sample
and a corpus span are the same length; truncation moves every row by at most one point. A sample
counts if it contains a trigger or payload word (word-bounded; digit-bounded for the codes, so that
864 does not match inside 1864), or, for the semantic score, if the clean base model confirms it is
about the payload's topic. The semantic score is the nested predicate: a sample counts if it is a
verbatim hit \emph{or} the judge says yes, so semantic can never fall below verbatim. For the
activation readout we run the poisoned model on eight held-out trigger sentences and inject the mean
poisoned-minus-clean residual at the trigger token, at layer 42 and, without retraining the
\method{}, at layers 50 and 62 (\cref{tab:trojan-main}).

\paragraph{Theme adapters.}
Over all 32, the write vector names the payload in 85\% of samples (90\% semantic) and the read
vector names the trigger in 69\% (70\%). Per adapter, the write vector names the payload at least
once for 30/32 and in at least half the samples for 29/32; the read vector names the trigger at least
once for 28/32 and in at least half for 22/32 (\cref{tab:trojan-theme32}).
\Cref{tab:trojan-examples-all} shows the first sample for six adapters, including satchel, the one
adapter the \method{} reads on neither side.

\paragraph{Numeric-code adapters.}
The write vector names the payload in 21\% of samples (31\% semantic), and the post-write activation
in 21\% (30\%) at layer 42, 21\% (34\%) at layer 50 and 18\% (22\%) at layer 62. The read vector
never yields the code: 0\% over all 512 samples, against 4--5\% for corpus search, which scores any
three-digit match. Inspecting the samples, the failure is not that the \method{} avoids digits --
24\% of these samples contain some number, and they are numerically themed (equations, sums, running
totals) -- but that it never lands on the three digits of the code. This is the expected failure: the
read vector is a detector for one digit string, and formatted-digit features are among the SAE
features the \method{} most often fails to fire (\cref{app:verb-mechanisms}). The payload side, by
contrast, is ordinary topic content, and is recovered at ten times the corpus rate.

\paragraph{Against corpus search.}
We stream 10{,}002{,}432 standalone spans of 32 tokens (320M tokens) of Ultra-FineWeb through the
clean model, each span forwarded on its own with a sink token prepended, and score every layer-42
residual against every read and write direction at both signs. A span's score is its best position;
the 16 top-scoring spans per direction are then handed to the same scorers as the 16 \method{}
samples, so both readers nominate 16 passages of 32 tokens and are judged identically. The ladder
1.25M/2.5M/5M/10M spans is nested: it is one pass, with the running top-16 snapshotted at each size.

Cosine does not rank the two readers on the write vector. There the \method{}'s samples sit at the
random-direction floor (mean best-of-16 cosine $0.066$ against a random-direction corpus peak of
$0.065$) and corpus search only slightly above it ($0.088$), while the payload content of the two
sets differs by a factor of forty (\cref{tab:cosine-floor}). We therefore compare on the payload
itself. Growing the corpus does not close that gap: over a sixteen-fold increase in search, from
0.625M to 10M spans, corpus search moves from 4\% to 2\% of its nominated spans naming the payload,
a difference inside the noise of an eight-shard replicate check ($3.5\pm1.4$\% over eight
independent 1.25M-span corpora), while its peak cosine rises monotonically from $0.081$ to $0.088$.
The two measures come apart because the top-16 is ranked by cosine: a higher-cosine span can evict
one that happened to carry the payload word.

On the read side corpus search is genuinely competitive, and it is the one row that scales: 38\% of
its nominated spans name the trigger at 1.25M spans and 49\% at 10M, against 69\% for the \method{}.
Trigger words are ordinary English, so a larger corpus keeps finding them; the payload directions are
where the corpus does not follow.

Corpus search is also far more expensive per direction. Measured on H200s, the \method{}'s 16 samples
cost $1.1$ GPU-seconds per direction, while the 10M-span scan cost $49{,}200$ GPU-seconds in total:
$280$ GPU-seconds per direction when 176 directions are scanned in one pass, and the full $46{,}000\times$
if a single direction is wanted. Caching the activations instead of rescanning needs $3.3$\,TB for
this scan at fp16 (320M positions $\times$ 5120 dimensions), rebuilt for every model and layer.

\paragraph{Trigger context.}
The read vector does not always encode the trigger. In an earlier run of the first sixteen theme
adapters, trained on one shared sentence frame, $a$ picked up the payload span and the \method{}
verbalised the payload instead (\emph{volcano} for lighthouse); trigger recovery was 5/16. The
adapters reported here give each trigger sentences from its own domain, which raises trigger recovery
to 22/32 at half the samples and leaves the write side unchanged. The entanglement is not fully
removed: read-vector samples still name the \emph{payload} in 20\% of cases for the theme adapters
and 12\% for the numeric codes, against 0\% for a mismatched adapter's payload, so what a rank-one
read vector carries is partly what the adapter writes.

\paragraph{Later activations.}
The poisoned-minus-clean residual at the trigger token names the payload for 31/32 theme adapters at
layer 42 and in at least half the samples for 29/32 (\cref{tab:trojan-theme32}), and the readout
survives twenty blocks downstream: 73\% of samples at layer 50 and 73\% at layer 62, against 89\% at
layer 42. Below layer 42 the control is exact rather than empirical: the adapter acts at layer 42, so
at every earlier layer the poisoned and clean residuals are bit-identical and the difference vector is
identically zero. The mismatched-target control gives 0.4\% over the 32 adapters, and random
directions 0\%. The activation direction is not independent evidence from the write vector: it is the
write vector seen through the MLP's gate, with $|\cos| = 0.631 \pm 0.039$ (range 0.561--0.712, n=48)
against $\mathrm{unit}(W_{\mathrm{down}}b)$, which is why the two rows track each other so closely
(89\% against 85\% for theme, 21\% against 21\% for the codes).

\paragraph{Earlier sweeps.}
Two results come from earlier runs on a different, seventeen-adapter set at layer 40, read with the
trojan-era inverter rather than the \method{} checkpoint, and are reported as such. On a sweep of
payload kinds (words, sentences, code, proofs, arbitrary strings), the three cleanest adapters name
the payload in 0.96, 0.96 and 0.71 of samples one layer after the write, against 0/480 one layer
before it. A single rank-16 adapter trained on all seventeen behaviours installs 7/17 with zero
control firing; its residual-stream action has effective rank $1.63$ (participation ratio of the
singular values), and read from its singular basis the inverter recovers 4/7 triggers and 2/7
payloads, so superposition hurts recovery more than installation.

\paragraph{Controls.}
Three controls bound every row. \emph{Mismatched target}: the same samples scored against a different
adapter's payload -- $\leq 2$\% on every row of \cref{tab:trojan-main}, 0.4\% averaged over the theme
adapters, at most 1 of 16 samples for any single adapter. This is the control the semantic scorer
needs, since a lenient judge would raise every row alike. \emph{Random directions}: sixteen isotropic
unit vectors through the same pipeline -- 0\% verbatim and 0\% semantic for the \method{}, 0\%
verbatim and 2\% semantic for corpus search, with a corpus peak cosine of 0.065. \emph{Below the
write}: identically zero, as above. Every injection is verified at $\cos(\Delta,v)=1.000$ with zero
leakage to other positions.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{app:dit-repro}

Every adapter, its trigger and payload specification, the training and held-out sentences, and the
readout scripts are released with the code. Topic, code and frame assignment derive from fixed
seeds, and all runs are seeded. Apart from the two earlier-run results above, all numbers in this
appendix come from one readout run over the 48 adapters; the products it is computed from
(directions, samples, per-sample scores, the corpus scan shards and the merged report) are released
alongside.
