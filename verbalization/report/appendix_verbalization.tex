\section{MAEMM expression of SAE Features}
\label{app:verb}

For a held-out SAE feature $f$ we inject $v = \mathrm{unit}(W_{\mathrm{enc}}[:,f])$ at layer~1 on a
marker token ($h \leftarrow h + \lVert h \rVert \, v$), generate best-of-$b$, then re-read each
generated text through the \emph{clean base model with the adapter disabled}. Disabling the adapter
at read time is load-bearing: the model cannot satisfy the metric by encoding something only its own
LoRA recognises. Every generated text is scored against all $65{,}536$ features rather than only its
paired one, which yields each feature's activation on text generated for \emph{other} features at no
extra cost. That control is necessary here: a direction-agnostic baseline --- the same RL fed random
isotropic directions --- reaches approximately the same aggregate score, so the metric is largely
gameable by generic fluent text.

\subsection{Criteria}
\label{app:verb-measure}

Everything is a threshold on one number, and the threshold is not innocuous. The inherited absolute
bar of $1.0$ has no derivation: it spans $1.8\%$ to $561\%$ of a feature's own corpus peak, and
$9.2\%$ of features have a corpus peak below it. $\mathrm{norm\_act}$ is unimodal (mode $0.51$, no
trough), so there is no natural cut either.

\subsection{Rare-feature training}
\label{app:verb-rare}

Strong targets do exist for rare features: mining $10$M tokens gives every one of $9{,}972$ TRAIN
features a full top-16, and the rarest band's rank-0 median activation is $84.3$ against a whole-SAE
median corpus peak of ${\sim}103$. The old bank's problem was scan budget, not corpus scarcity. We
LoRA-finetune on the resulting $159{,}552$ pairs and evaluate on $1{,}520$ features never trained on.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Criterion & SFT init & RL & After rare-training \\
\midrule
$>$ own \texttt{null\_p95} (permissive)        & 0.270 & \textbf{0.514} & 0.458 \\
$>$ own \texttt{null\_max} (promiscuity-proof) & 0.100 & \textbf{0.235} & 0.178 \\
$\geq$ top-16 corpus example (strict)          & 0.030 & \textbf{0.074} & 0.046 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Held-out TEST. Rare-feature training improves on its own initialization and generalizes to
features never trained on, but does not reach the RL checkpoint, which received no rare-feature
supervision. It also makes the model more promiscuous: median signal-to-null falls $6.09 \rightarrow
3.33$ against RL's $14.76$, which is why the permissive criterion reports $+18.8$ points of transfer
and the strict one $+7.8$.}
\label{tab:verb-rare}
\end{table}

\subsection{What the failures are}
\label{app:verb-failures}

Of the $966$ features no arm activates, $85$ fire on a token only when a near-constant continuation
follows and $14$ have example sets consisting of one repeated boilerplate string. Both are ill-posed
targets rather than misses --- ${\sim}10\%$ of the set, a floor no training removes. Classifying
\emph{every} evaluated feature by its modal peak token, so the comparison is enrichment rather than
prevalence, the failure rate rises monotonically from content toward structure
(\cref{tab:fail-by-token-class}), identically under all three criteria.

\subsection{The failures are expressible}
\label{app:verb-human}

Calling these features inexpressible would be a claim about the features; the measurement only
supports a claim about this inverter. Given a feature's corpus windows, an LLM writes an activating
passage for it readily (\cref{tab:verb-human}), so a textual preimage exists and is findable --- the
limit is identification from the injected direction, not text production.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Set & Features & Sentences & Median \% of corpus peak \\
\midrule
Inverter scores 0.00       & 21 & 30 (29 fire) & 89 \\
\emph{No} arm activates    &  8 & 16 (16 fire) & 91 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Hand-written sentences for features the inverter cannot activate. The second row is the
harder set --- no arm fires them under any conditioning --- and includes two purely positional
numeric features, a digit inside a suite number and inside a company registration number, reached at
$98\%$ and $96\%$.}
\label{tab:verb-human}
\end{table}

To separate lack of exposure from something structural, we cluster the $925$ unactivatable features
by their corpus examples and train on $811$ (prose, news, web UI, disclaimers, metadata), holding out
$113$ of entirely unseen types (recipes, sports, contact blocks) with zero overlap.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrcc}
\toprule
Training & Features & TRAIN $\mathrm{norm\_act}$ & TEST $\mathrm{norm\_act}$ \\
\midrule
none                          & --- & 0.004 & 0.008 \\
16 spans each                 & 811 & \textbf{0.344} & 0.035 \\
64 spans each                 & 811 & \textbf{0.387} & 0.031 \\
64 spans, decoder-conditioned & 811 & \textbf{0.352} & 0.049 \\
\midrule
decoder-conditioned, \emph{no} training & --- & --- & \textbf{0.181} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Cluster-disjoint transfer. Trained features go from producing nothing to a third of corpus
peak; held-out features stay at median $\mathrm{best\_act}$ $0.00$ with $78\%$ producing zero, and
their null-beating rate falls \emph{below} baseline ($0.221 \rightarrow 0.195$). Quadrupling the
spans improves the fit and slightly worsens transfer. The last row is the sharpest result: decoder
conditioning with no fine-tuning beats every fine-tuned arm on held-out features, so training on the
$811$ costs the model direction-reading it already had.}
\label{tab:verb-transfer}
\end{table}

The MAEMM is trained only on encoder directions, yet reads the decoder row $W_{\mathrm{dec}}[f]$ --- a
different object, encoder--decoder cosine $0.49$--$0.67$ --- zero-shot. On the $155$ features the
encoder handles it reaches $\mathrm{norm\_act}$ $0.828$ against the encoder's $0.965$; on the $113$
it fails, $0.181$ against $0.008$, with $35\%$ beating $\mathrm{null\_max}$ against the encoder's
$0\%$. Nor is the model merely guessing on what it cannot fire: across $96$ rarity-matched features
at $b=32$, self-consistency is indistinguishable between hard and easy groups ($0.757$ vs.\ $0.754$,
$p = 0.93$) against a cross-feature baseline of $0.516$. It produces one confident cluster of text
pointing elsewhere, which is a worse failure for interpretability than not knowing, because the
output still reads like an explanation.
