An inverter samples many texts for one feature, and how much those samples differ is a property of
the method in its own right. Too little variety would mean the MAEMM has found one reading of the
feature, or one template, and repeats it; too much would mean each sample is a separate guess. We
measure where it sits on the $2{,}000$ features of the 131k-SAE evaluation set, against the
feature's own corpus windows and against an LLM, first in wording and then in meaning.

\paragraph{Wording.} For each feature we take the mean, over all pairs of its texts, of the Jaccard
similarity of their token-trigram sets. The MAEMM (rl-last16) has median $0.043$
(\cref{fig:verb-diversity}, left), and only $3$ of $2{,}000$ features reach the range of
boilerplate templates ($\geq 0.40$). Pairs of texts written for \emph{different} features score
$5 \times 10^{-5}$, three orders of magnitude lower, so what repeats is specific to the feature. The
MAEMM nevertheless repeats itself more than the corpus does. At equal length (first $32$ tokens)
its samples overlap $3.6\times$ as much as the feature's corpus windows ($0.043$ against $0.012$), and
$2.3\times$ as much with the opening $8$ tokens, which its samples often share, removed as well.

\paragraph{Meaning.} Word overlap cannot tell a paraphrase from a different reading, nor varied text
from text that is not about the feature, so we also embed every text and read each feature's $k=8$
texts two ways (\cref{fig:verb-diversity-embed}a). \emph{Spread} is the Vendi score, the effective
number of distinct texts among the eight. \emph{Specificity} is how much more similar a feature's
texts are to each other than to texts for other features. The MAEMM is the most specific source
($0.31$, against $0.19$ for both the corpus top-8 and claude-opus-5) but less spread than either:
its eight samples amount to $2.2$ distinct texts, against $2.9$ for the corpus top-8 and $3.4$ for
the LLM. It
also covers less of the feature's other contexts (\cref{tab:verb-diversity-embed}). Unseen corpus
windows for the feature lie closer to the corpus top-8 or to the LLM's texts than to the MAEMM's.
All three orderings hold under a second, unrelated embedding model, and spread and specificity
also hold with every text cut to $32$ tokens (\cref{app:verb-diversity}). The MAEMM gives a sharp
but narrow reading of each feature. The narrowness is shared by the other activation-to-text model
we can compare against. NLA-AV, which reads the same injected direction and writes an explanation of
it, gives $2.0$ distinct texts on a random $500$ of these features, where the MAEMM gives $2.2$ on
the same features. Its explanations are less specific ($0.17$), because they share one scaffold
across features: explanations of \emph{different} features already sit at cosine $0.67$.

\paragraph{Agreement tracks success.} The narrowing is not collapse onto a wrong answer. Features on
which the MAEMM's samples agree most are the ones it expresses best
(\cref{fig:verb-diversity-embed}b): the unverbalized share ($\texttt{norm\_act} < 0.10$) rises from
$4.4\%$ in its most-agreeing quartile to $19.2\%$ in its most-spread one, and agreement separates
success from failure at AUC $0.66$ ($0.61$ by word overlap; \cref{fig:verb-diversity}, right). That is
too weak to use as a detector, and it is consistent with the failures of \cref{app:verb-human}: where
the MAEMM misses, it still produces a coherent cluster of text, only a looser one.

\paragraph{Spread alone is not the goal.} A rare-feature LoRA that damaged the model shows why
spread and specificity have to be read together. It is the most spread source ($4.2$ distinct
texts of eight, $0.0003$ word overlap), but barely specific ($0.05$), so its variety comes from not
being about the feature, and it fires less (median $\texttt{norm\_act}$ $0.52$ against $0.85$; $74\%$
against $88\%$ of features at best-of-8). Its own agreement is the strongest predictor of its success
anywhere here: median $\texttt{norm\_act}$ $0.83$ on the features where its samples agree most, $0.12$
where they spread most (AUC $0.77$). The pattern is not one bad run: both later rare-feature
training arms show it too, one trained on a rare-weighted draw ($4.2$ distinct texts, specificity
$0.04$) and one on this set's own failure clusters ($4.4$ and $0.02$, read on its $1{,}805$ untrained
features), against the MAEMM's $2.2$ and $0.31$.

Taken together, the MAEMM is not templated, and it is the most feature-specific of the sources, but
it offers a narrower view of each feature than its own corpus evidence or an LLM reading that
evidence: about two distinct readings in eight samples where they offer three. Whether its samples
agree is weak evidence that it has found the feature.
