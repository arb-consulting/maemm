A single MAEMM generalizes to direction families it never saw in training. It does not generalize
to everything. A substantial minority of SAE features resist every intervention we tried, and this
section characterizes them.

\paragraph{Scoring.} Throughout, $\texttt{norm\_act}$ is a feature's best activation over $b$
generated samples as a fraction of its peak over the stored corpus windows, read in the frozen
subject model with the inverter's adapter disabled. $\texttt{null\_max}$ is its best activation on
text generated for \emph{other} features --- a per-feature control that is free, because every text
is scored against all $65{,}536$ features rather than only its paired one.

\paragraph{What we tried.}
Corpus sparsity predicts difficulty, so the first hypothesis is that the targets were too weak. They
were: scanning ten times more corpus gives every rare feature a genuine peak rather than a lucky
hit, and retraining on features resampled inversely by firing rate does reduce the failing set
(\cref{app:verb-rare}). It does not close it, and it does not reach the RL checkpoint that received
no rare-feature supervision at all. We then trained the MAEMM directly on the failing features, with
sixteen and then sixty-four mined spans each, and separately changed the injected vector from the
SAE encoder direction to the decoder row. Every one of these moves what it touches and none makes
the class transfer: training on $811$ failing features raises them from $0.004$ to $0.387$ mean
$\texttt{norm\_act}$ while $113$ held-out features of unseen types stay at $0.031$
(\cref{tab:verb-transfer}). Quadrupling the spans improves the fit and slightly worsens transfer,
and fine-tuning under decoder conditioning leaves held-out performance ($0.049$) well below the
same conditioning with no fine-tuning at all ($0.181$).

\paragraph{What resists.}
The residue is not arbitrary. Sorting all $1{,}520$ evaluated features by the token at which they
peak (\cref{tab:fail-by-token-class}), the failure rate climbs monotonically as that token moves from
content toward structure, under all three criteria. What survives every intervention is the class of
features individuated by position and continuation rather than by topic: a feature firing on a
function word, a mid-word fragment, punctuation or a digit. About $10\%$ of these are ill-posed
targets rather than misses --- they encode a fixed collocation, or their whole example set is one
boilerplate string (\cref{tab:verb-taxonomy}) --- but that leaves the great majority unexplained.

\paragraph{The limit is the MAEMM's, not the feature's.}
Two controls separate the two readings. First, we sample $40$ features the MAEMM fails on strictly
($\texttt{best\_act} \leq \texttt{null\_max}$), show an off-the-shelf LLM only their corpus windows,
and ask it to write activating text under the MAEMM's own best-of-4 budget
(\cref{tab:llm-baseline}). Every feature fires, $97.5\%$ beat their own null, and the median reaches
$87\%$ of corpus peak --- against a MAEMM median of $0.00$ on the same features. A textual preimage
therefore exists and is cheaply findable given the corpus evidence, which the MAEMM never sees.
Second, on the features it cannot fire the MAEMM is not guessing: its samples are as mutually
self-consistent as on features it handles easily (\cref{app:verb-consistency}). It produces one
confident cluster of text pointing somewhere else --- a worse failure for interpretability than an
obvious one, because the output still reads like an explanation.

Taken together: for this inverter, a structurally identifiable class of SAE features is effectively
unlearnable. It is not that the features are meaningless, that the corpus lacks evidence for them, or
that the model has not been shown enough of them --- each of those was tested and none accounts for
the result. What the MAEMM appears to have learned is thematic reconstruction, and these features are
not individuated thematically. We state this as a property of one inverter warm-started on
${\sim}10^6$ topic-like directions; establishing an intrinsic limit would require the same failure to
survive across inverters trained expressly to cover these feature types.
